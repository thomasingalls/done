# done — Active slice

**This file is disposable, and that is its function.** It holds the slice of work that is active right now
and nothing else. Nothing of record value lives here: decisions go to [decisions.md](./decisions.md), and
closed work goes to [completed.md](./completed.md).

## How this file works

**Everything below the rule is replaced wholesale when the active slice changes.** Not amended, not struck
through, not archived in place — deleted and rewritten. A working document that accretes is a working
document nobody reads, and the two files that _are_ records already exist.

**One thing must happen first.** When a slice closes, write its outcome into
[completed.md](./completed.md) _before_ rewriting this file. That ordering is the only thing protecting
the record, because after the rewrite the evidence is gone.

**It grows a tree when the slice deepens.** If work goes deeper without the slice changing — an item turns
out to have four parts, one of which has three — nest it. Nesting is free and says something true about
the shape of the work. What is _not_ allowed is a second top-level slice: two active slices means the file
has stopped answering the question it exists to answer, and one of them is really next-up.

**Status vocabulary**, deliberately small: **not started** · **in flight** · **done** · **blocked** (which
must name what it is blocked on).

**Being wrong here is cheap.** This file is allowed to lag by a day. It is not allowed to describe a slice
that is no longer active.

- **Row 1 — the algorithm suite and crypto-agility.** selected — construction with vectors, in
  [selections/row-1-algorithm-suite.md](./selections/row-1-algorithm-suite.md).
  - **Suite 1: AES-256-GCM on every asynchronous path, XChaCha20-Poly1305 on the synchronous one**, HKDF-SHA-256,
    Ed25519, HPKE base with X25519, SHA-256. **Half of this was decided by row 2 and nobody noticed**: §13.10
    forbids sharing a primitive across the two paths, row 2 spent the sync slot on ChaCha, and WebCrypto offers
    exactly one AEAD. The platform decided the rest.
  - **Agility is the half that was genuinely open: a carrier version selects the whole suite.** No per-record
    algorithm field, so no negotiation and nothing to downgrade — and every carrier authenticates its own
    version, so a renumbered record fails to open rather than selecting a weaker suite.
  - **The §13.2 separation rule is machine-checked, not asserted.** The generator buckets every role by path and
    fails if any primitive appears in both. Four published KATs (GCM Test Case 16, RFC 5869, RFC 8032, FIPS 180-4)
    run against WebCrypto on every invocation.
  - ~~**Open:** Ed25519's availability is asserted, not verified.~~ **Closed** — the floor is Baseline

- **Row 2 — the synchronous journal AEAD and its nonce discipline.** selected — construction with vectors

  seal is built, measured on the handset that raised it, and both the selection and the budget stand.
  - **XChaCha20-Poly1305; 24 random bytes per record; no counter, no durable state.** The width is the
    point: a 96-bit nonce would also hold at this journal's volumes, and that is the objection to it — the
    guarantee would become a claim about application behaviour that a later change can silently invalidate,
    and nonce-reuse faults are retroactive and silent.
  - **The vectors prove themselves.** `test/vectors/journal-aead.json` — the draft-irtf-cfrg-xchacha §A.3.1
    KAT, four composition vectors, nine negatives. The generator round-trips every vector and asserts every
    negative fails, on each run, so a "must fail" list cannot quietly stop failing.
  - **The browser benchmark exists now** — `test/latency/journal.spec.ts`, recorded in

    day it was found.** The breach was real — 145 µs and 295 µs at 18 B and 419 B against 1 ms, and **1 930 µs
    at 4 kB**, reproducing within 1.4% across two runs and surviving a drift check, so a payload effect and not
    a warm phone. **The WASM seal closes it at every payload: 35 µs, 65 µs and 240 µs p95**, the last with 4.2×
    of margin, measured on the same handset in a session whose JS baseline reproduces the breach to 0.8%. TRD

  - **The payload qualifier is still open, and that was the point of not revising the budget.** Large pastes
    and large documents remain outside what the spec treats. Declining to settle it early left the question

    within **1%** of `@noble` at 4 kB, so pure-JS arithmetic was the wall and swapping the import was never a

    wasm-bindgen, base64-inlined, instantiated synchronously — and it **beats libsodium**, the ceiling that
    priced the option: 45.3 µs against 51.8 at 4 kB, on 1/34th of the bundle.
  - ~~**A Rust-compiled module measures 24.3 kB gzipped base64-inlined, leaving 19.4 kB spare.**~~ **That
    figure was a proxy and it was wrong by 6×.** It weighed a package exposing only the 12-byte-nonce
    construction, where row 2 selected the 24-byte nonce and the width is load-bearing. The real module is
    **4.5 kB gzipped through the bundler — 0.7 kB _less_ than the `@noble/ciphers` it replaces.** The whole
    question had been framed as buying speed with bytes, and there is no trade.
  - **Optimising for size was spending the wrong currency**, and the first build got it wrong: `opt-level = "z"`
    reached only 1.17× over `@noble` — a module that would have fitted and not fixed the defect. Size barely

  - **Two things TRD §12 asserted are checked rather than trusted.** Compilation does have a synchronous form —

  - **What remains is a criterion rather than a construction**, and it is the only such item in 3a's scope:

    [performance.md](./performance.md) and [decisions.md](./decisions.md).
  - **A side result that vindicates a selection.** XChaCha's extra HChaCha20 block costs 1.02× at 4 kB, so

  - **The emulated profile was wrong, not conservative, and that is the transferable finding.** The 4× CPU
    throttle predicted 266 µs at 4 kB against an actual 1 498 µs. Against desktop the phone runs the **cipher**

    assumed. No amount of care with that extrapolation would have caught this.

- **Row 3 — the encrypted record and local key-envelope formats.** selected — construction with vectors, in
  [selections/row-3-record-format.md](./selections/row-3-record-format.md).
  - **Both formats, because they are one construction a level apart** — the envelope holds the key that derives
    the subkey that opens the records, and §6.1's start-up order makes the dependency literal.
  - **Every cleartext field is in the AAD, and `kind` is why it matters.** IndexedDB holds a structured clone, so
    `v`, `gen`, `seq` and `kind` are editable properties that the load path trusts. A `state` record supersedes
    everything before it, so an editable `kind` is a way to make the store delete its own history through its own
    trim path.
  - **`principal_id` is bound because two principals on one profile is the ordinary path**, not an attack —
    PRD §6.7's anonymous Local workspace plus an account workspace, with no second human involved.
  - **The nonce argument has a different shape from row 2's, and is answered rather than waived.** 12 random
    bytes under NIST SP 800-38D's 2^32-per-key ceiling for random IVs. Row 2 rejected a conditional argument
    because nothing could check the condition; here `seq` already bounds it conservatively in one comparison.
    **This pins something §6.1 left open: `seq` does not reset at trim.**
  - **Eleven negatives, and the must-fail list cannot drift** — the generator compares what it published against
    what it actually asserted, in both directions.
- **Row 14 — recovery-code entropy, generation, step-up authentication and confirmation UX.** selected,
  storage-facing part — construction with vectors, in
  [selections/row-14-recovery-code.md](./selections/row-14-recovery-code.md).
  - **125 bits, Crockford Base32, five groups of five, and no stretching at all.** The row's stated
    question — _does this need Argon2id?_ — turned out to be answered by a sentence PRD §7.6 had already
    written while rejecting passwords: _server-held sealed data permits offline guessing_. That fixes the
    threat model, and what was left was a number.
  - **Stretching is declined on the same grounds row 2 declined a 96-bit nonce.** It converts entropy you
    do not have into time you hope an attacker will not spend; at 125 generated bits there is no deficit,
    and adding a work factor would trade an unconditional argument for one that decays with hardware.
    It also costs a WASM dependency against 43.7 kB of headroom, and this way costs nothing.
  - **The entropy claim is machine-checked rather than asserted.** The generator counts the preimages of
    every symbol under `byte & 0x1f` and requires exactly eight — 256 = 8 × 32 — so a changed alphabet or
    a modulo where a mask belongs fails the build instead of silently producing weaker codes that still
    seal and still open.
  - **The keypair in the vectors checks itself.** It is RFC 7748 §6.1's, and the generator derives the
    public key from the private scalar and refuses to write the file unless they match. An AAD that binds
    a public key to a private one proves nothing if the vector's own halves are unrelated.
  - **Step-up is a bound challenge, not a freshness window**, and that half is drafted for **review 3b**
    rather than closed here — TRD §15 splits row 14 between the reviews and the document respects the split.
- **Row 13 — device-lock derivation, enrollment migration and copy.** selected — construction with vectors,
  in [selections/row-13-device-lock.md](./selections/row-13-device-lock.md). **In review 3a's scope but not
  in Stage 1's path**, so nothing but the 24th put a date on it.
  - **A generated 125-bit code always; WebAuthn PRF, where available, as an additional seal of the same
    key; a user-chosen passphrase rejected.** §13.3's "PRF- or high-entropy-code-derived" reads as a menu
    and is not one. **Row 14's deciding sentence applies here with _server-held_ deleted** — the seal sits
    in the profile beside the ciphertext it protects, so for the attacker this feature exists to resist,
    guessing is offline and free. Disk encryptors accept a memorable secret because a TPM or a Secure
    Enclave rate-limits it; **a browser origin's only equivalent anchor is the authenticator.**
  - **Making the code the root rather than the fallback is §13.4's own value reused** — _PRF's optionality
    is not a product branch_ — and PRD §7.6 already builds the recovery keypair that way. One enrollment
    flow, one failure mode, no device-dependent surface. A random `L_lock` in between means adding,
    replacing or removing a factor is one 32-byte seal that moves no content and no content key.
  - **The §13.4 separation has to live inside the PRF evaluation input, and the natural reading does not.**
    One assertion plus four HKDF labels leaves whoever holds that output holding all four roles' keys — an
    assertion approved for _unlock your signing key_ yields the material that opens content. Four
    evaluation inputs give four unrelated outputs. The property is about the bytes the authenticator
    returns, so **the vectors are the only place it is checkable at all.**
  - **Enrollment must rotate the content key, and that is the row's finding.** Resealing the envelopes
    leaves `K_g` unchanged, so a profile copy taken _before_ enrollment — an OS backup, the synced
    directory §13.3 names — keeps opening it, and therefore keeps opening content written _after_ the lock
    was enabled. Without rotation the lock buys its intended audience almost nothing while telling them
    otherwise. **This is the part most likely to come back changed from 3a**: rotation is unilateral and
    free until Stage 4 gives the workspace other devices.
  - ~~**Open:** the PRF availability floor is asserted, not verified.~~ **Closed** — once for both, as the
    row asked, and **the answer for PRF is that there is no floor to state**: its availability is a matrix of
    OS, browser and authenticator kind rather than a version. That is a result in the construction's favour —
    a capability this ragged is precisely one that cannot be a root, which is what §5 already argued.

### 2. Draft TRD §15's protocol-review scope — in flight

Rows 4–12, 15–17, 20, and the network-facing part of 14. **Ten of the thirteen are now selected** — each taken
as TRD §15 named it next — on the same terms 3a's scope was closed: a construction with vectors rather than an
intention, generated by a self-verifying script, checked for staleness and for AAD-field coverage by `pnpm check`.

**This stopped being the item that blocks nothing.** 3b is now booked for the same week as 3a (item 4), so the
rows still open in its scope have the same working days that 3a's had — and TRD §13.11 holds each review until
its own scope is closed.

- **Row 5 — the finalization shape.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-5-finalization.md](./selections/row-5-finalization.md). This is what makes revocation hold
  against a hostile relay, and it was the guarantee most completely specified-but-not-designed.
  - **An append-only per-credential chain of SHA-256 links, with service-signed heads amortized over a batch.**
    `final: true` is the cutoff. A separate hot acceptance-signing key `K_accept`, certified by the
    authorization key in the epoch, because evidence-before-apply puts whatever signs heads on the delivery
    path and the key that admits and revokes credentials should not have to be there.
  - **§7.5's three candidates were two, and that is worth more than the selection.** Written side by side, the
    checkpoint-plus-suffix candidate _is_ the chain with a checkpoint carrying the head vector, and
    per-envelope receipts are the chain with the chain removed. The real question was never three-way: it was
    whether evidence for position _n_ says anything about position _n-1_. **A bag of independent receipts has
    the defect that every subset of it is as well-formed as the whole** — §7.5's own argument against a numeric
    high-water mark, one level along.
  - **Two carriers gained a field**, and both are Stage 4 rather than Stage 1 because neither is on-disk: the
    epoch payload and the checkpoint manifest each carry the accepted-head vector. Folded into §13.5 and §7.6.
  - **Non-equivocation is bounded and the bound is now written down.** The chain is non-equivocating per
    credential and within one client's view; a service running two consistent forks is undetectable by
    cryptography alone. A transparency log or gossip would close it and is declined as a larger commitment than
    the rest of the design. This is §13.5's treatment of rollback applied to the same class of problem, and
    saying so was the point — _non-equivocating_ is true of the chain and reads as though it were true of the
    service.
  - **One thing it turned up that is not a selection: §12 has no row for the latency it creates.** An envelope
    arriving ahead of the head covering it is held, so **remote edit → visible on the second device** is
    bounded below by head cadence — a service parameter. §12's `envelope verify on receipt < 2 ms` measures the
    client, which is not where the cost is. **No row was added**, because §12 is build-failing thresholds and
    there is nothing to measure yet; it is recorded so it is not discovered after it is missed.
- **Row 11 — which device-to-device ceremony.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-11-enrollment-ceremony.md](./selections/row-11-enrollment-ceremony.md). §15 wanted it early
  because it changes what 3b is reviewing.
  - **A short authentication string over an in-band agreement, nonces committed before revealed, five Crockford
    Base32 symbols displayed on the new device and typed into the existing one.** The wrap's HPKE `info` binds
    the transcript hash, so a wrap made for one ceremony opens under no other.
  - **QR is declined as the root on row 13's own reasoning, applied a third time.** A ragged capability cannot
    be a root: `BarcodeDetector` is in neither Safari nor Firefox, so depending on it means shipping a JS
    decoder against **43.7 kB of headroom that row 2's WASM candidate already wants 24.3 kB of** — competing
    with the fix for a measured defect. And the direction is wrong, since the device that must be convinced is
    the laptop. It survives as an accelerator of the same transcript, same bits, same failure mode.
  - **Typed rather than compared is the product decision inside the crypto one.** A comparison is satisfied by
    pressing the affirmative button; a transcription is not. It costs one input field row 14 already built, at
    one group of five instead of five.
  - **The commitment is demonstrated rather than asserted, and that is the vector worth reading.** The
    generator grinds the substituted key's nonce and **forces the honest ceremony's first two symbols in 566
    tries**; all five is the same search at 2²⁵. Without commit-reveal the string carries **zero** bits against
    a service that speaks second, which is arithmetic rather than emphasis.
  - **What Stage 4 owes and 3b should look at**: the mismatch copy, which has to be honest about an attack on a
    content key without alarming the overwhelmingly likely mistyped character — row 13's two-texts problem in
    reverse.

- **Row 4 — the envelope wire format and batching granularity.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-4-envelope-format.md](./selections/row-4-envelope-format.md). §7.4 had already decided the
  field split as a product constraint, so this row is an encoding — **except for one field that is not.**
  - **Two bound field lists, because one of them structurally cannot do the whole job.** The nonce is the AEAD's
    own input and the digest is a function of the ciphertext the AAD helps produce, so neither can sit in the
    AAD — and both are fields a reader trusts. The signature exists to reach exactly those two. Row 3's rule
    (any cleartext field a reader trusts is authenticated) survives intact; what changes is that satisfying it
    takes two lists rather than one.
  - **`seq` is the credential's outbound position and its accepted position, as one number, and row 5 forces
    it.** Row 5's first client rule is _verify a head against the link you replayed yourself_, and a client can
    only replay unaided if it computes each position itself. **Two numbers would quietly undo row 5.** The cost
    is that a client cannot skip: a refused envelope stalls that credential's chain until it is re-minted at the
    same position.
  - **`digest` is of the ciphertext**, so the relay's whole validation is one hash and one signature over a
    header — which is what §7.2's content-blindness has to mean if it is real.
  - **Batching costs no attribution granularity**, which sounds like it should. One envelope carries one actor
    by construction, so merging two of the same actor's updates loses nothing that was ever recorded per update.
  - **Nothing here needed a second pass**, which is worth noting only because rows 5 and 9 both did, for the
    same reason: a check that is not a cryptographic binding passes a coverage audit while testing nothing. This
    construction has none.
- **Row 9 — chain-head discovery for a brand-new device.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-9-chain-head-discovery.md](./selections/row-9-chain-head-discovery.md). §13.5 said there is no
  mechanism that fixes this, and there still is not; **row 5 is what made it load-bearing rather than a
  footnote**, since the whole accepted-chain guarantee is stated relative to a head the client believes.
  - **Two of the three arrival paths are not brand-new, and interrogating the premise was most of the work.**
    Enrollment has another device in the room; migration and archive restore travel in a user-held package. Only
    recovery with no surviving device genuinely asks — and it is rare, deliberate, and already a screen the
    person is reading.
  - **So row 11 is amended one commit after being selected**, and its transcript now binds the epoch head beside
    the epoch number. The five typed symbols authenticate where the lineage is as well as whose key it is, at a
    cost of two bytes each side. **Nothing has been built, so it cost a regenerated vector file and a changed
    short string** — after the first deployment it would have cost a protocol version.
  - **The nonce moves replay from _anyone in the path_ to _the authorization service itself_**, which is §7.3's
    own boundary. Without it a head assertion is a standing artifact any intermediary can re-serve forever, and
    replay needs no key at all.
  - **The residual is bounded by §7.5 rather than by anything added here.** Revocation is atomic with rotation,
    so a hidden revocation is a hidden generation — a stale head cannot come with current content. The attack
    degrades to a partition, which is availability, and whose symptom is the one thing a person running a
    recovery is looking at. **It is a bound and not a guarantee**, weakest on a workspace that has been quiet
    since the rotation, and it says so.
  - **The coverage audit refused the first pass, on the field the row exists for.** The obvious replay negative
    fails on a string comparison between the nonce sent and the nonce carried — **which fails whether or not the
    nonce is signed at all.** The isolating negative rewrites the carried nonce to match, so only the signature
    can refuse it, which is also the real attack. Three rows after it was built, the audit caught the thing it
    was built for.

- **Row 8 — the attribution-preserving representation.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-8-attribution-record.md](./selections/row-8-attribution-record.md). It had to precede row 7,
  and §15 said so: a checkpoint must preserve attribution, and nothing yet said what that attribution was made
  of.
  - **The problem was a retention floor, not a missing signature.** §7.4's envelope already makes attribution
    trustworthy. What was open is verifying it later without keeping every key ever used — and §10.4 recorded
    that floor as **unbounded in practice**, since nothing retires a card and every claim needs the generation
    that sealed its envelope.
  - **A standing signed claim, minted by the writing credential at card creation, living in the content root.**
    §13.7 forbids extracting a claim from an envelope later, and §7.4 lets a credential sign only its own
    operations — **between them the minting moment is fixed** and no checkpointing device can produce these
    afterwards.
  - **The residence is the actual result, and §7.6 had already written it while talking about something else**:
    attribution evidence must be portable workspace material, checkpointed and restored with the workspace
    rather than held in a separate channel. Taken literally that says _put it in the content root_ — which
    deletes the separate store, the sixth subkey, the re-encryption machinery and the separate migration path.
    **§6.1's trim re-seals it under the current generation for free**, and the unbounded floor closes.
  - **The body commitment makes the claim falsifiable, not unforgeable.** The card id already stops a record
    being moved. What the commitment buys is a distinction the product cannot currently draw: a card created by
    one actor and since rewritten wholesale still shows its creator. `verified` and `exact` are reported
    separately, so **provenance mode owes three states rather than two.**
  - **The envelope position is deliberately not bound**, which is the one place row 3's take-the-free-binding
    rule was declined. Verifying it would tie every claim to row 5's chain and force chain retention — **a
    binding whose verification forces retention is not free in a row that exists to reduce retention** — and it
    is unnecessary, since a record only reaches the content root through an accepted envelope.
- **Row 7 — checkpoint construction, cadence and retention.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-7-checkpoint.md](./selections/row-7-checkpoint.md).
  - **§7.6 sets three constraints and only two are solved here.** No merged state, and no bare state vector as a
    coverage claim, are this row's. **No checkpoint that re-signs another credential's operations is row 8's** —
    with claims signed by their own authors inside the snapshotted content, the producer signs the container and
    nothing in it, and the forbidden shape stops being prohibited and becomes unreachable. That is the whole
    argument for the ordering, and it held.
  - **One forced cadence rule that is easy to miss.** A checkpoint is openable only while its generation is
    retained, so **a rotation must produce a checkpoint under the new generation before the old one may be
    dropped** — otherwise tidying up destroys the recovery path. It is an ordering constraint between two
    operations that look independent, and it is found at a restore rather than at a review.
  - **Retention is two, because §11.3 test 8 says so.** A policy of one makes that test unpassable by
    construction — worth checking a policy against its own tests before writing it rather than after.
  - **`sv` and `ds` are bound separately and opaquely.** Separately because either alone is exactly the claim
    §7.6 says is not a coverage claim; opaquely because their content is Yjs's and §10.1's deferred v13 → v14
    upgrade would otherwise change this format too.
  - **The head-vector sort is load-bearing rather than tidy.** Two devices checkpointing the same accepted state
    must produce the same digest, and a map in insertion order would not — the disagreement would read as a
    corrupt manifest rather than a canonicalization bug.

- **Row 6 — the anti-replay rule and envelope retention depth.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-6-antireplay-retention.md](./selections/row-6-antireplay-retention.md). **This is the row that
  closes the sync core**, and three inbound constraints had to be reconciled into one depth rather than one.
  - **Four conditions, not five, and deleting the fifth is the result.** §7.5's _no second link at a position
    already held_ reads as a condition needing a log of every link ever admitted. It is implied by the position
    check over a monotonic mark: a second envelope at _n_ arrives when the mark is at _n_ or beyond. **So the
    carrier is one `(seq, link)` per credential** rather than a structure that grows with every accepted write.
  - **The mark carries a link and not just a number, and §7.5 already wrote that sentence one level up.** _A
    numeric high-water mark is not a finalization boundary_ — one position down, it is not an anti-replay state
    either, and there is a negative for the client that advanced a counter without keeping what it replayed.
  - **The theorem is the first vector, and it reads as bookkeeping.** A chain replayed from genesis and the same
    chain replayed from a checkpoint's head vector reach the same link at the same position. That equality is
    the whole justification for every retention floor below: the chain has no memory beyond its last link, so a
    checkpoint's head vector **is a chain state** and not a summary of one.
  - **Row 3 had already paid for the carrier.** It bound `kind` in the record AAD against a stated attack, and
    that binding is what lets a third `kind` arrive at no cost and what stops a heads record being reinterpreted
    as a `state` record and swept into the trim. No sixth subkey, no new format — row 8's move at a smaller
    object.
  - **§7.4's retention depth was one checkpoint too shallow.** It said _back to the last checkpoint it has seen
    admitted_; §7.6 retains two because §11.3 test 8 falls back to the next-newest, and §14.3 has each device
    re-envelop its own suffix from the checkpoint it applied. Compose them and a device trimmed to the newest
    cannot supply what the fallback needs. **The correction needs no protocol field** — a device counts two
    locally — and the failure appears at a restore rather than at a review.
  - **The obvious recovery from row 4's stall breaks the chain permanently, and that is a `must not happen`.** A
    client that minted position 7, waited, heard nothing and re-minted over fresher content produces a second
    link at a position the service may already have accepted. Every other device refuses it as a compromised
    credential. **An honest client frames itself**, and nothing distinguishes it from the attack. A definite
    refusal licenses a re-mint; a timeout does not.
  - **One residual is published as a demonstration rather than a negative, because it succeeds.** A heads record
    rolls back to an earlier, genuinely sealed one — an AAD authenticates a record and cannot order two valid
    ones. It routes into §7.5's stated equivocation bound rather than adding a new one.
- **Row 10 — the terminal and portable cutover-package formats.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-10-portable-package.md](./selections/row-10-portable-package.md).
  - **§15 asked for two formats and there is one, and the TRD had already said so twice.** §14.4 describes the
    service _populating the portable package_ rather than producing a second artifact, and its offline replica
    keeps a live head until _importing the package later **upgrades** its knowledge_. Add §9.3's fidelity
    archive and it is **one format at §13.5's three lineage states** — never established, live, retired. The
    saving is that §11.3 tests 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 exercise one code path.
  - **The real work was §14.2's one clause: the old trust root, _bound so it cannot be substituted_.** Every
    other item in that list is service-signed, so a forger supplying their own root re-signs the epochs, the
    credentials come from the epochs, and row 8's claims verify under the credentials. **A signature over the
    package does not help, because the same substitution replaces whatever signed it.**
  - **So the chain terminates in the one thing §13.8 makes mandatory.** The recovery code opens `R_priv`;
    `R_priv` opens the generation wraps; **so the root is bound in the HPKE `info` of every wrap.** A
    substituted root derives a different key schedule and opens nothing. **Two fields in an `info` that had none
    specified** — no new key, no new signature scheme, no new secret, row 14 untouched.
  - **And it is needed by exactly one reader, which is row 9's argument a fourth time.** A surviving device knows
    the root from enrollment; an enrolling device gets it through the person. Recovery with no surviving device
    is the case that asks — and the case that must hold the code. **The anchor and its audience arrive
    together.**
  - **Row 8 deleted a part.** §14.2 item 3 implied an attribution part with its own digest and retention rule;
    with the claims in the content root they are inside the checkpoint already carried. Item 4's generations are
    _not_ deleted by it — row 8 closed the attribution floor, and the package still needs generations to open
    the ciphertext, which §13.7 already warns is a different question.
  - **HPKE is implemented here rather than pinned, and that is a change from row 11.** Row 11 pinned its `info`
    because its claim was about the transcript; this row's claim is inside the seal, so a pinned string would be
    asserted rather than tested and the coverage audit could not reach it. The generator **reproduces RFC 9180
    A.1.1 before using it**, every invocation.
  - **One thing it changes that is not in its scope:** [sequencing.md](./sequencing.md) §5.4 prices the
    fidelity-archive route as _pulls a Stage 5 item into Stage 2_. That price is smaller than written — the
    archive is one state of a format Stage 4 builds regardless. **It does not decide §5.4**, whose other half is
    about attribution rather than cost.

- **Row 12 — the account credential and the local slot verifier.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-12-account-credential.md](./selections/row-12-account-credential.md). The last row of 3b's
  scope with cryptography in it, and **the third row whose apparent question was not its real one.**
  - **The verifier checks a signature, not a secret**, so §13.10's Argon2id candidate answers _none_ for the
    third time and on the strongest of the three arguments. Rows 13 and 14 said none because a generated
    125-bit code leaves no entropy deficit; here **there is no secret in the profile at all**, and grinding the
    verifier means forging Ed25519. The account credential is a WebAuthn discoverable credential, offered as
    `[-8 Ed25519, -7 ES256]`.
  - **Half of it had already been decided in TRD §16.1, which nobody was reading as a selection.** The
    inventory row says the service stores "Account authenticator **public material** and slot-verifier
    parameters", visibility "None — public material". A password verifier is a server-held secret, not public
    material. **A data inventory turns out to be a design constraint rather than a compliance artifact**, and it
    answered a question the design had not reached. Row 1's "half of this was decided by row 2 and nobody
    noticed", at a different pair of documents.
  - **The offline-guessing clause protects the network credential, not the slot**, and that is why it sits in
    §13.9 rather than in §6. A grindable local verifier would let a profile holder recover the thing that
    authenticates to the _service_, which §13.2 forbids this artifact supplying offline. The local gate against
    the person at a shared keyboard is a lesser thing, and §13.3's scoping sentence already covers it.
  - **Removing a secret introduces a substitutable public key, and that is where the work went.** The slot index
    is a structured clone whose properties the load path trusts, so an unbound public key is one property edit
    from a slot that opens under the attacker's own passkey — row 3's rule at a different store. Each credential
    gets a **service-signed binding**, so substitution needs the service's private key rather than write access
    to IndexedDB. **The gate ends up stronger than the custody beside it**: `L_local` yields to anyone who can
    run the origin and the binding does not.
  - **One role can root on the authenticator where §13.3's could not, and the reason is not a change of mind.**
    PRF is an _extension_ whose availability is a matrix; a signature is the core of the API, and this row uses
    no extension. Underneath that: the device lock needs _key material_ and this row needs a _proof_ —
    **a signature is checkable offline without being guessable offline; a derived key is not.** So one of
    §13.4's four factor roles turns out not to consume a factor output, and row 13's reserved
    `done/slot-verifier-prf/v1` label is now recorded as unused. A regenerated comment string, no vector value,
    on row 11's precedent.
  - **Row 1's agility rule bends once, and ES256 joins the suite.** WebAuthn credential creation _is_ a
    negotiation — the RP offers, the authenticator picks — so this is the one role the suite does not choose.
    **What replaces _no algorithm field_ is _one algorithm field, signed, written once_**, frozen at creation
    inside the binding. **This is the first time a 3b row has amended a 3a row**, and TRD §15 says so rather
    than leaving it to be noticed on the 24th.
  - **The coverage audit deleted a field and corrected two more, on the argument the row exists for.** `alg` was
    bound beside the key and isolated by nothing — the algorithm is a function of the key encoding, so it cannot
    be varied independently, and **a field that cannot be varied independently is not an independent binding.**
    Storing SubjectPublicKeyInfo fixed it with nothing argued at the audit. `rp_id` and `public_key` failed for
    the neighbouring reason — read by the verifier _and_ bound in the message, so the obvious negative fails
    twice and proves neither — and each needed a _consistent_ ceremony for the substituted value, which is what
    the real attacker would supply. **`aad-only-binding-public-key` is the negative the whole row exists to
    justify, and its first version was passing for the wrong reason.** Row 9's finding, a fourth time.
  - **The open question about which review holds it is answered rather than reassigned.** It looked
    storage-shaped because _offline-guessing resistance_ implied a secret at rest; there is none, and what the
    row contains is network-facing throughout. **It stays in 3b and 3a's closed scope stays closed**, which is
    the outcome worth having three working days out.

- **Row 15 — interrupted-rotation and downgrade behaviour.** selected and folded, in
  [selections/row-15-transitions.md](./selections/row-15-transitions.md). **The row's subject turned out to be a
  class rather than an operation**, and TRD §11.2 test 11 had already listed it without saying so.
  - **Every item in test 11 is a multi-step transition whose interrupted middle reads as a completed _lesser_
    transition.** A rotation that did not settle reads as no rotation being needed; an unsettled revocation reads
    as still authorized; a deliberate downgrade reads as account deletion. **So _fail closed_ is one property
    rather than five**: the intermediate state must be representable, distinguishable and resolvable, and must
    never be a valid representation of an endpoint. That is why there is **one intent-and-outcome protocol at
    three kinds** rather than three constructions arguing the same argument.
  - **PRD §6.10's sentence turned out to be a binding requirement rather than interface guidance.** _Deliberate
    downgrade must not silently become account deletion_ reads as a warning about a screen; a `rotate` and a
    `retire` intent are **the same eight fields** differing in `kind`, so an unbound `kind` is a way to submit
    one and commit the other. **Row 3's editable-`kind` finding at a third object**, and one level larger: there
    a store deletes its own history, here a lapse retires a lineage. The vectors publish the two messages side
    by side with the byte at which they diverge, because the claim is easier to believe as a diff.
  - **Row 6's outbound rule extends by one case, and the third case is why the refusal must be signed.** Row 6
    had two states — definite refusal, or silence. A transition has three, because it carries an **exclusion
    set**: a client that loses the race must _discard and rebuild_, not resubmit, since its exclusion set was
    computed against an epoch that has moved and resubmitting would revoke the wrong credential.
  - **That closes a gap row 6 left, and left by the rule rather than by oversight.** Row 6 said a definite
    refusal licenses a re-mint and never said where a definite refusal comes from. **Here a fabricated one makes
    an honest client discard a live content key** and rebuild against an epoch the attacker timed — repeated, a
    rotation that never completes, so a credential the owner is excluding stays able to read while the interface
    reports progress. **That is test 11's confidentiality promise changing silently, with no key material at
    all.** So the outcome is signed and **everything that is not a verified outcome is silence** — a timeout, a
    reset, an unsigned error body. Folded back into §7.4, where row 6's rule lives.
  - **A client that cannot reach the service stays pending, and that is the fail-closed direction.** Timing out
    and rebuilding is the same attack performed by the network. It is a liveness cost and it is accepted.
  - **The rotation promise is narrower than the obvious one, and the product owes the narrow version.** A device
    that has not observed the commit keeps writing under the old generation, and §11.2 test 6 requires admitting
    a late old-epoch update from a still-authorized offline device — so an excluded credential can read those
    writes. It cannot be closed without a device refusing to accept edits while possibly stale, which is §7.1
    inverted. **PRD §6.5's _removal governs the future_ was not quite the whole sentence**, and the remainder is
    now written there: from the moment a device learns of the revocation, what _that device_ writes is closed.
  - **Row 7's cadence rule is violated by exactly this class**, and the retention condition is three rather than
    one: settled, a checkpoint under the new generation admitted, and a local state record under it. The obvious
    implementation clears the obligation when the epoch commits, which is before the checkpoint exists — row 7's
    own warning about being found at a restore rather than at a review.
  - **Two fields are absent for the third instance of one rule.** No `from_gen`, because the epoch already binds
    the current generation; no `state` flag, because whether you won is `committed_id == your id`. Row 10 left
    out a part count and row 12 an algorithm field on the same argument: **a second binding of a derivable fact
    is a thing that can disagree with it.**
  - **The coverage audit refused three passes and the third is worth keeping.** Four intent negatives failed
    twice, because the settlement rule compares four fields across both messages — **row 12's fix, needed a
    second time**, which suggests a field read on both sides of a comparison is the ordinary case. Then
    `aad-only-outcome-committed-id` was rescued by two fields: the substituted id was the rival rotation's, and
    the rival differs from the base in `payload_digest` alone, so eliding that field made the two ids equal and
    the negative passed for a reason unrelated to the signature. **A negative built from a near neighbour is
    isolated by the neighbourhood.** Fixed by substituting an id two fields away.

**One piece was already drafted before this**: row 14's step-up construction, in
[selections/row-14-recovery-code.md](./selections/row-14-recovery-code.md) §5, along with the two things it
hands to 3b — whether the service rate-limits envelope fetch, and the atomic-replace semantics at the service
boundary.

**Three rows left in the scope: 16, 17, and the network-facing half of 14 — and none of them is design work.**
With rows 6, 12 and 15 the substance closes: envelope, acceptance, retention, checkpoint, attribution,
enrollment, discovery, the package that carries all of it out, the credential the account is reached by, and the
rule for what happens when any of it is interrupted.

- **Row 16 does not arise.** It is conditional on PRD §11's pricing question, which is deferred and which no
  sentence may depend on, so what it needs is to be _told to the reviewer as conditional_ rather than closed.
- **Row 17 is a published profile and a version**, and TRD §13.11's own table already owes it to Stage 0
  independently of 3b.
- **The network half of 14 is drafted**, in row 14 §5, **and row 15 closed one of its two halves** — the
  atomic-replace semantics, which are now `kind: 'replace-recovery'`. What is left of it is the step-up
  construction, which needs folding rather than designing.

**Nothing cryptographic is left in 3b's scope**, which is a different sentence from _the scope is closed_ and is
the one that is true.

**One thing the fold turned up that is not a selection: §15's own header had drifted behind its table.** It read
_nine rows are selected and ten are open_ while the tables held eleven and eleven. Corrected to thirteen and
nine, with the note that rows 14 and 20 appear in both halves because each is split — which is what made the
count ambiguous enough to drift in the first place.

**A note on folding, since 3a's fold was a batch and this was not.** All four rows went into the TRD as they
were taken, rather than waiting for the scope to close. The early-warning sign that fired last time — _five
