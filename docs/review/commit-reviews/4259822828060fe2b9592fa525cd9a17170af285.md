## Original-contract trace

The commit answers OC-077's interrupted-rotation/downgrade choice and sharpens OC-017/OC-029's committed
revocation semantics. Binding `kind` carries OC-020's difference between a live dormant lineage and retirement.
Signed outcomes extend OC-032's rule that silence or relay assertion is not acceptance evidence, while old-generation
retention preserves OC-030/OC-033's checkpoint and attribution recovery floors.

## Construction and state-machine effect

Clients persist an Ed25519-signed intent before sending it. Its id is SHA-256 of version, kind, workspace,
lineage, parent epoch, target, and canonical payload digest. The service commits at most one id per
`(lineage,kind,target)` and signs an outcome containing kind, workspace, lineage, target, epoch, and committed id.
Silence resubmits identical bytes; an outcome naming the client's id moves pending → applying → settled; another
id moves pending → stale → rebuild. No target-generation content is written before a verified winning outcome,
and the old generation is retained until settlement, a successor checkpoint is admitted, and local trim holds
successor state.

## Assumptions and guarantee boundaries

The protocol assumes canonical sorting makes payload digests reproducible, transition targets are monotone/unique,
the authorization successor and outcome key can be verified through the retained lineage, and pending intent bytes
survive crashes. A partitioned client intentionally stays pending without a timeout. Revocation excludes future
content only after a device observes the commit; offline authorized devices may continue writing under the old
generation, and an excluded credential may read those writes if it obtains their ciphertext. No remote wipe or
instant global cutoff is promised.

## Evidence and oracle independence

The generator checks fixed keypairs, deterministic intent/outcome bytes, cross-kind divergence, canonical payload
sorting, positive signatures, and field-isolating negatives. It exercises the three-way decision as modeled, but
the producer and verifier are one project-owned generator and no service, durable key store, fault injector, or
concurrent multi-client implementation exists. Generated vectors establish encoding consistency, not atomic commit
or crash recovery.

## Downstream propagation

## Consequences and residual failures

Signed outcomes prevent a relay from fabricating “superseded” and inducing an honest client to discard a live key.
Binding kind prevents rotate/replace/retire confusion. Availability deliberately fails closed under silence, perhaps
indefinitely, and user copy/support timing remains open. A crash-safe pending store, server transaction boundary,
concurrent winner retrieval, and checkpoint/trim settlement are specified obligations without implementation evidence.

## Falsification attempt and counterevidence

I tested timeout-based rebuild as a liveness improvement; it reproduces the forged-refusal attack because the
network can force key discard, so indefinite pending is the honest fail-closed result. I tested resubmitting a
losing rotation at the successor epoch; its exclusion set was built against the parent and can revoke the wrong
credential, so discard/rebuild is necessary. I tested whether an unsigned refusal is sufficient; because it licenses
discarding a live key, it is not. No counterexample breaks the selected abstract state machine, but none of these
tests proves the eventual durable/service implementation.

## Nuance preservation

The commit improves propagation by narrowing the product promise to devices that have observed the revocation and
by stating the offline propagation window. It preserves the difference among confidentiality, availability, and
past-copy erasure, and carries row 7's checkpoint obligation into settlement rather than clearing it on epoch commit.

## Verdict

Supported with qualifications. The unified intent/outcome protocol coherently resolves the original failure class
and its signed three-way state is well justified. The conclusion remains design-time: persistent atomicity,
concurrency, fault injection, and user-visible indefinite pending behavior must still be demonstrated.

## Findings and unresolved checks

No standalone finding survived. Required later checks are crash injection before/after intent persistence, lost
outcomes, concurrent winners, successor verification, and old-generation deletion only after all three settlement
conditions.
