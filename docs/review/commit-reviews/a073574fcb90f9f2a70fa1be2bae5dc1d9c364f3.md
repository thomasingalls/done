## Construction and state-machine effect

### Accepted chain

Each credential has a gap-free SHA-256 chain over workspace, credential, position, envelope epoch/digest and prior
link. `K_accept`, certified by the authorization epoch but separate from `K_auth`, signs a head at position `n` over
the client's computed link, epoch and `final` bit. A client walks its own view, applies a run only when a head covers
the terminal recomputed link, refuses a second link at a held position, and holds input above the newest head.
`final: true` is the committed no-future-position boundary. Epochs and checkpoints later carry the same head vector.

### Enrollment

Existing and new devices each commit to a role-labelled 32-byte nonce before either reveals it. The transcript binds
workspace, epoch, device credential ids, new wrapping key and ordered nonces. HKDF maps it to five uniform Crockford
Base32 symbols; the new device displays them and the person types them into the existing device. The HPKE wrap's
`info` binds the transcript hash. QR may carry the same transcript but cannot replace transcription as the root.
Commit `992bc91` later adds `epoch_head` and regenerates the SAS, a cheap pre-deployment amendment.

## Assumptions and guarantee boundaries

- Accepted-chain non-equivocation holds within one client view. A service can maintain consistent split views until
  an epoch, checkpoint, device comparison, gossip, or transparency mechanism makes views meet.
- Missing heads or withheld positions stop synchronization; this is the intentionally accepted availability failure.
- `K_accept` remains a hot online key. Separating it limits authorization-key exposure but does not make the
  acceptance service non-malicious.
- Head cadence creates a remote-edit-visible latency term not covered by the existing client verify budget.
- SAS resistance is 25 bits per completed ceremony and depends on both commitments preceding reveals and the person
  actually transcribing. Abort/retry/product behavior remains relevant.
- The enrollment generator pins the transcript and wrap `info`; it does not implement HPKE or integrated service/UI.
- At this commit, the SAS binds epoch number but not the head; `992bc91` is required to prevent stale-head pairing on
  enrollment before deployment.

## Consequences and residual failures

One head amortizes acceptance evidence over a whole run while preserving the client's own replay as the source of
truth. Separate acceptance/authorization keys are a sound exposure reduction, and the explicit split-view bound
prevents “non-equivocating” from becoming a global-service claim. The typed committed SAS avoids the familiar
approve-button bypass and prevents a second revealer from grinding after seeing the honest nonce; QR optionality does
not fork the security protocol.

Residuals are hot-key compromise, service fork until views meet, hold latency/availability, unmeasured head cadence,
human error/retry, absent HPKE/client/service integration, and the initial missing head binding repaired one commit
later. Current PRD §§6.3 and 6.5 still instruct readers that both choices are open.
