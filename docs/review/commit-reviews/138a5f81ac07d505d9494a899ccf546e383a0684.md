## Original-contract trace

This is empirical follow-up to `OC-025` and `OC-064`: the selected synchronous construction must meet
the low-end write budget. It also tests the original warning in `OC-051` that a deferred harness can
hide the only evidence capable of changing a selection before implementation.

## Construction and state-machine effect

No shipping construction changes. The harness compares selected Noble XChaCha, stablelib XChaCha,
libsodium WASM XChaCha, and Noble narrow-nonce ChaCha through one warm-up/batching/single-operation
routine. Three comparable implementations must reproduce the published project vector. Fresh random
nonces remain inside the measured operation. The narrow-nonce row is explicitly a cost reference, not
an admissible replacement.

The projection subtracts the measured Noble seal from the existing full write and adds the libsodium
seal: about 15, 44, and 274 microseconds at 18 B, 419 B, and 4 kB. It assumes framing/base64/storage
costs remain independent of the implementation.

## Assumptions and guarantee boundaries

- The conclusion is about the recorded Pixel 9a, browser build, locked versions, and three payloads.
  Two similar pure-JS results reject a simple import swap; they do not prove every JavaScript
  implementation or engine is bounded by the language.
- Means compare implementation throughput. They do not supply the p95 full-write statistic required by
  §12, which remains inherited from the preceding journal-device run and projected composition.
- Vector equality establishes wire compatibility for one published input, not full implementation
  equivalence or auditability.
- WASM speed shows a useful ceiling; libsodium's package size and asynchronous readiness make it a proxy,
  not the selected fix.
- Raw device samples and the recorded user agent land under ignored `test-results/`; the committed ledger
  has the device label/date/means but not the raw distribution or full OS/browser identity.

## Evidence and oracle independence

The expected ciphertext comes from the existing row-2 artifact rather than from the first candidate,
and stablelib/libsodium provide implementation diversity for that one KAT. All candidates share the
same project harness, device session, inputs, timing API, and summary procedure. The exact code and locked
package versions survive, but the original Pixel run cannot be rerun in this workspace and its raw samples
are not committed.

The numeric discriminators are internally reproducible: stablelib differs from Noble by about 0.8% at
4 kB, and Noble is about 24.2 times slower than libsodium. The projected full-write values follow from
the previous level table. The reported 37 ms readiness value is not an exact readiness timestamp because
the page queries it only after fetch and agreement work.

## Downstream propagation

`6b021c4` uses the WASM ceiling to price bundle categories; `9b49b74` later builds a purpose-specific

## Consequences and residual failures

On the recorded phone, changing from Noble to stablelib would not close the 4 kB breach, while WASM has
ample compute margin. This justifies investigating WASM without revising the wire format. It does not
generalize the JS-language claim to other devices/engines, establish the bundle fit, or implement crash
safety. The readiness race/timestamp weakens only the 37 ms startup number; libsodium is independently
non-adoptable and the throughput comparison occurs after readiness.

## Falsification attempt and counterevidence

- I recomputed the ratios and projected whole-write arithmetic; each matches the tables within rounding.
- I checked candidate agreement: the three XChaCha implementations compare against the published
  ciphertext, while the 12-byte-nonce construction is correctly excluded.
- I challenged “language, not library” with the sample breadth. Two JS libraries on one ARM/V8 target
  disconfirm one-library pathology there, but do not establish a universal language theorem; the prose's
  “on this hardware” qualifier is load-bearing.
- I traced readiness ordering and found `agree()` precedes `await candidate.ready()`, so the 37 ms value
  cannot mean the exact moment libsodium became callable.

## Nuance preservation

The performance record mostly preserves the necessary boundaries: same wire construction, one phone,
proxy rather than candidate, means rather than honest p95, projected rather than implemented full write,
and wider nonce selected for non-performance reasons. The subject and some working prose overcompress
the one-device result into “the language,” while the detailed text restores the qualifier.

## Verdict

`supported-with-qualifications`: the paired pure-JS comparison is a good discriminator against a cheap
library swap on the recorded hardware, and WASM's throughput margin is large. Generalization, raw device
provenance, full-write p95, bundle fit, and the readiness timing remain outside what this experiment proves.
