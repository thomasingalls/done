# Commit `ff0b48f` — Give the step-up a nonce, and bind the transition rather than the key

## Scope and exact sources

Inspected the full patch and exact objects at `ff0b48f8ce7f1878ac6abb9c849380800b55370c`: `docs/TRD.md`,
`docs/selections/row-14-recovery-code.md`, `scripts/generate-recovery-vectors.mjs`,
`scripts/generate-account-credential-vectors.mjs`, `scripts/lib/aad-coverage.mjs`, `scripts/lib/webauthn.mjs`,
`test/vectors/recovery-code.json`, `docs/decisions.md`, and `docs/progress.md`. External check: W3C WebAuthn
Level 3 Candidate Recommendation Snapshot, 26 May 2026, §§7.2 and 13.4.9,
https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/ (retrieved 2026-08-21).

## Change seen while moving backward

Returning to `d357664` removes the 32-byte service nonce, replaces the bound row-15 transition id with the
earlier deterministic target description, removes four field-isolating step-up negatives, duplicates the
WebAuthn ceremony instead of sharing it, and reopens row 14's network half. The earlier challenge could be
reproduced indefinitely from operation fields and therefore did not make “single-use” true.

## Original-contract trace

The change directly answers OC-076's recovery step-up construction and OC-082's vector gate. Binding the
transition `kind` through its id carries OC-020/OC-077's requirement that a replacement or downgrade cannot
silently become retirement. It also consumes row 12's account credential (OC-074) without making account
authentication itself a content-key delivery path (OC-034).

## Construction and state-machine effect

The service issues `nonce[32]`; the client derives
`SHA-256(LP(label)||LP(v)||LP(intent_id)||LP(nonce))`; the account credential signs the WebAuthn ceremony; and
the service verifies the intended transition and retires the nonce. Binding the row-15 id transitively binds
kind, lineage, parent epoch, target, and payload digest, including the new recovery public key. This removes a
second independently editable key binding and prevents cross-kind redirection. Durable storage and crash
recovery for the service's issued/retired-nonce set are required by the guarantee but remain an implementation
obligation rather than a selected persistent record here.

## Assumptions and guarantee boundaries

The construction assumes the transition id is computed exactly as row 15 selects, the service issues an
unpredictable nonce and remembers its retirement, the account can have multiple valid passkeys, and the device
already opens every generation it must rewrap. It deliberately does not require the old recovery code. A party
with both device content access and an account credential can therefore replace the recovery path and cause an
availability loss, but gains no new confidentiality capability. The guarantee is operation freshness and
purpose binding, not remote proof of user intent or durable nonce-state implementation.

## Evidence and oracle independence

The generator runs a positive assertion plus a wrong passkey and four field-isolating negatives. Each
field-negative rewrites `clientDataJSON` to the expected challenge, so refusal reaches signature verification
rather than stopping at the challenge string comparison. The AAD-elision runner demonstrates that each named
field rescues an associated negative when omitted. Ed25519 verification uses WebCrypto and fixed RFC 8032 key
material, but the challenge producer, verifier, and vector oracle remain project-owned JavaScript sharing one
model. W3C WebAuthn independently supports checking type, challenge, expected origin, RP-ID hash, flags, and
signature; it also requires a policy decision for `crossOrigin`/`topOrigin`, which these verifiers omit.

## Downstream propagation

The new challenge reaches TRD §§13.8, 13.9, 13.11, and 15; the row-14 selection and recovery vector; progress
and decisions; and the shared ceremony used by row 12. It closes review 3b's register scope. The current PRD's
statement that step-up remains open was not updated, establishing the row-14 portion of candidate
`f-tip-prd-stale-selection-status` at this commit; rows 5 and 11 await B-02 tracing.

## Consequences and residual failures

Replay of an assertion from a retired nonce no longer verifies for a fresh attempt, and binding the transition
id prevents a recovery replacement assertion from authorizing retirement. Availability still depends on the
service issuing and durably retiring nonces, and on the transition service remaining reachable. A valid
cross-origin WebAuthn assertion is accepted without checking whether its top-level embedding origin is allowed;
that boundary is recorded separately in `f-b9ff071-webauthn-cross-origin-policy`.

## Falsification attempt and counterevidence

I tested the claim against three counterexamples. An unmodified old assertion stops only at the challenge
comparison, so it is correctly published as a demonstration rather than cryptographic evidence. Rewriting the
old `clientDataJSON` to the new challenge causes signature failure, isolating the nonce binding. Replacing the
transition id likewise reaches signature failure, so redirecting `replace-recovery` to `retire` is rejected
provided row 15's id is correct. The counterexample that survives is a correctly signed assertion with
`crossOrigin: true` and an unapproved `topOrigin`: neither verifier reads those members although WebAuthn §7.2
requires the RP to validate that policy.

## Nuance preservation

The commit improves the earlier draft by naming freshness as nonce state rather than a time window and by
binding the whole transition rather than duplicating its key. It preserves the explicit availability residual
of recovery replacement. Nuance is lost in two propagation points: the PRD still says step-up is open, and the
shared WebAuthn verifier describes a complete ceremony while omitting the cross-origin policy fields.

## Verdict

Supported with qualifications. The nonce and transition-id correction addresses the demonstrated replay and
cross-kind problems, and the negative design is materially better than string-comparison-only tests. Completion
still depends on durable service nonce semantics, independent/integrated conformance tests, PRD propagation, and
an explicit WebAuthn cross-origin policy.

## Findings and unresolved checks

- Existing candidate `f-tip-prd-stale-selection-status`: row 14's stale PRD status begins here.
- New candidate `f-b9ff071-webauthn-cross-origin-policy`: row 12 introduced, and this commit reused, a verifier
  that ignores `crossOrigin` and `topOrigin`.
- Later implementation must make nonce issuance/retirement crash-safe and atomic enough that restart cannot
  silently turn a retired challenge back into a standing artifact.
